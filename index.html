09 Video Walkthrough1

Procore BIM · YouTube

Making BIM intuitive for every construction team

Procore BIM makes models accessible to everyone in the field, while 2D views, dynamic measuring and AR support faster QA/QC and earlier issue detection.

Read more

Procore BIM has been crucial to us because it’s easy. There is no one I can’t hand the model to, and they can intuitively start figuring out how to get their way around.

When I can get my 6-year-old to get on this Procore BIM app and he’s flying around, then he asks, “Can I keep playing with that?” That’s a lot of fun. I’m like, “If he can get it, that means everybody can get it.” At every level, that is the tool to get in the hands of people in the field, especially because most people already have access to Procore in varying capacities.

When you open up the model to everybody invited to your project, you are taking steps towards a BIM-enabled workforce because there’s no excuse.

When I log into a model and see a lot of names of people I don’t know flying around with their little blue dots, that confirms success. We got it to the people who needed it, and the right people are accessing all this information.

The thing that stood out most to me was the 2D views and dynamic measuring. The first time I saw that tool and how it worked was the first time I felt like you could truly build from the model.

Clicking on the same wall in the Procore model, flattening it out, and being able to pull my own measurements from the floor up to it—or to adjacent blockouts or pipe penetrations—was an “oh wow” moment for our superintendents.

They can truly verify that sleeves are in the right spot and that they are not installing things incorrectly that could later create problems during MEP rough-in. Being able to take 3D, flatten it back to 2D, look at it in the real world, and move seamlessly back and forth was the moment I thought, “I can’t believe we have this. This is awesome.”

Procore AR has elevated our ability to perform QA/QC checks much faster. Before, it was more like: stand in the right spot, hold the model in the correct position, switch to perspective view, and pan around to inspect.

But when you can overlay the model onto the real world and align it accurately, that is where the real value comes in. You can quickly see whether things are in the right spot—or identify problems before they become expensive.

You do not need all the back-and-forth checking. It speeds things up. It is a leaner way of working: less waste, earlier issue detection, and getting it right the first time.

People say, “Measure twice, cut once.” With VDC coordination, it feels like you are measuring 300 times because you review everything in multiple dimensions—and then you can confidently install it.
